Title: The Lantern Cartographers

Pip Lark is an original cartoon cartographer who lives in Lumenfold, a city drawn in bold ink where streets rearrange themselves whenever someone tells the truth too loudly. Pip carries a square lantern that does not shine light. It shines directions. When the lantern points at a blank corner of the world, a new road sketches itself in, one chalk line at a time.

The trouble starts when the three Night Inks leak out of the binding of the Great Atlas. They are not people and not monsters from any other story. They are original forces: Silt, who smudges coastlines until harbors forget the sea; Bramble, who grows clockwork orchards that tick fruit until it locks shut; and Quietus, who drinks the names off signposts so neighbors walk past one another. If all three finish their work, Lumenfold will stay beautiful and completely unreachable.

Pip cannot fix the map alone. The lantern only draws a road when two people disagree about where it should go and then choose a third path together. Pip's companions are original characters: Nia Quill, a bridge-singer whose voice can pin a moving street in place for one minute; Bram Holt, a retired storm-mailman who delivers clouds in paper envelopes; and Oddment, a small geometric fox made of folded tickets, who remembers every road that has ever been erased.

Movie 1, The Map That Forgot the Sea. Silt pulls the harbor inland. Fishing boats wake up in a meadow, and the tide rings like a bell under the cobblestones. Pip, Nia, Bram, and Oddment chase the shoreline before it dries into a straight line. The movie ends when they redraw the coast as a circle instead of a border, and Silt retreats into a single wet brushstroke locked inside a glass buoy.

Movie 2, The Clockwork Orchard. Bramble has planted ticking trees across the terraces above Lumenfold. Every hour, a gate closes forever. The companions climb through orchards that measure their doubts and lock the gates that match them. Nia loses her voice, Bram mails himself into a storm by mistake, and Oddment spends a whole reel as a blank ticket. They break the orchard by choosing a road that is late on purpose. The last tree opens, and Bramble is folded into a seed that cannot count.

Movie 3, The Ink Beneath the World. Quietus wipes the names from the city, then from the companions, then from the legend of the map itself. The only picture that still knows them is the square lantern, and its glass is cracking. The finale crosses the places of the first two movies — the round harbor and the opened orchard — and ends in the binding of the Great Atlas. Pip does not defeat Quietus by remembering harder. The four of them invent a new shared name for the city and speak it together. The roads settle. The lantern becomes an ordinary lamp. The last shot is Lumenfold, specific and findable, with the three captured inks asleep in the margin and the companions walking home by a road that no longer moves.

Tone: sincere adventure, comic timing, no horror, no real people, no celebrities, and no characters from any existing film, book, or game. Faces stay exaggerated, geometric, and hand-drawn. The trilogy should feel like a finished three-film cartoon, each film with its own beginning, middle, and ending, and a single quiet payoff at the end of the third.
